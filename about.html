Home / Our Story

45+ years of showing up for New Mexico.

Founded in 1979, Roadrunner Food Bank has grown into the state's largest hunger-relief distribution hub — and a proud member of the Feeding America network.

Mission

We move food to where hunger is, before it becomes a crisis.

Roadrunner Food Bank sources, warehouses, and ships food across New Mexico through a network of pantries, soup kitchens, shelters, schools, and senior centers. We don't just hand out food — we build the supply chain that lets hundreds of local partners do it every week, in every corner of the state.

Beyond distribution, we advocate for policy that keeps families fed, and we partner with health and social-service organizations to treat hunger as the public-health issue it is.

Vision

A New Mexico where no one goes hungry — and where access to food is treated as a basic right, not a privilege.

Core Values

Dignity, urgency, partnership, and stewardship — of every dollar, every pound of food, and every relationship with the people we serve.

How it works

One warehouse, hundreds of front doors.

Food banks source and store food at scale; local partner agencies are the front door where neighbors actually pick it up.

Source

Rescued grocery and farm surplus, government commodities, and purchased staples arrive at two hubs in Albuquerque and Las Cruces.

Sort & Store

Volunteers and staff inspect, sort, and palletize donations in the warehouse, ready to ship within days.

Distribute

A fleet of trucks delivers to 200+ partner agencies, who run the pantries, meal sites, and mobile distributions people visit directly.

History

From one pastor's pickup truck to a statewide network.

In 1979, a local pastor who saw hunger up close began rescuing food headed for the dumpster and getting it to families who needed it. That small effort became the backbone of hunger relief in New Mexico.

1979

Founded to rescue surplus food from grocers and get it to New Mexicans facing hunger.

1980s–90s

Joins the national Feeding America network; expands warehousing and adds a Las Cruces hub to reach southern NM.

2000s

Launches senior, school-pantry, and mobile-distribution programs to reach people who can't easily get to a pantry.

Today

Warehouses tens of millions of pounds of food a year for 200+ partner agencies across all 33 New Mexico counties.

National network, local roots
